Mirage: Controlled Evaluation of Corruption-Mixture Training for Cross-Subject Motor-Imagery EEG

How well do EEG classifiers work for an unseen person, and what happens when the signal is disrupted?

This study compares CSP–LDA, EEGNet, ShallowConvNet, and ATCNet on the public BNCI2014-001 motor imagery dataset. It examines generalization to held-out participants, robustness to signal disruptions, the effect of augmentation, and performance with fewer electrodes.

Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) evaluation keeps each test participant outside model development. Results are summarized at participant level, with uncertainty estimates and multiple-comparison correction.

Selected study finding

+4.91

macro F1 points on disrupted signals

EEGNet improved with mixed-disruption training compared with standard training. Its average disrupted-data score improved for all nine participants.

95% CI: +3.09 to +6.89
Holm-adjusted p = 0.0313

Study design, findings & limitations

What the study tests

  • Clean and disrupted EEG from unseen participants, using four motor imagery classes and 22 recorded channels.
  • Noise, amplitude scaling, temporal loss, frequency-band attenuation, and random or targeted channel loss.
  • Standard versus augmented training, and separate retraining with 9, 6, or 3 electrodes.
  • Participant-level macro F1, accuracy, balanced accuracy, and Cohen’s kappa, with bootstrap intervals and corrected comparisons.

Availability: Manuscript submitted to IEEE ICASSP. The study code is not yet publicly available.
